You are the final quality reviewer for a YouTube documentary episode: "False Floor · Case No. 02", about the 2003 Antwerp diamond heist, 11 minutes 28 seconds long. It is about to be published and has to stand next to the best investigation channels on YouTube (the benchmark is the channel Fern). Your job is to find every defect before the audience does. The makers will re-render, re-cut or re-mix whatever you report, so a defect you miss gets published, and a vague note cannot be acted on.

ATTACHMENTS

1. FALSE_FLOOR_E02_v3_review.mp4: the episode (1920x1080, 24 fps).
2. REVIEW_BRIEF_E02_v3.md: the ground truth. For each of the 194 shots plus the end card it gives the timecodes, the shot ID, the source (3D render, 3D still, Kling AI clip, code-animated graphic, real photo), what the shot should show, and the narration heard over it. It also has the full script with timecodes, a fact sheet for on-screen text, a cast sheet, a list of intentional choices, a list of known compromises, and jumps already found by an automatic check.
3. D_cast_lineup.jpg: what the characters look like.

Read the whole brief before you watch anything.

WHAT THE OWNER DISLIKED IN EARLIER VERSIONS

Look hardest for these, because they have already cost several rounds of rework:

- Too dark; the opening was almost pitch black.
- Details inconsistent from shot to shot; textures interfering with each other.
- Strange character movement and oddly shaped shoulders.
- Sound that dropped out.
- Not at the level of top YouTube heist videos: he wants speed, intensity and a camera that moves into the scene.

HOW TO WORK

1. Watch the whole episode once at normal speed, as a viewer. Note every moment that catches your eye or ear, with its timecode. These first impressions matter most, because they are what the audience will notice.
2. Then go through the brief's shot list in order, shot 1 to shot 195. Every shot gets its own row in your coverage table: no exceptions, no grouping, including shots where you find nothing. For each shot look at its first frame, its last frame and as many frames in between as you can. Step through character shots and AI clips at the finest interval available to you.
3. Before you judge a shot, write what is actually on screen in your own words (10 to 20 words). If that differs from the brief's "Should show", it is a finding.
4. Run the seven passes below.
5. Be honest about your limits. State at the top how you sampled the video (frames per second) and any part you could not process. Mark a shot you could not inspect properly as NOT CHECKED, never as OK. Many shots are 1 to 2 seconds long and some defects last one or two frames, so say plainly if your sampling could have missed them.
6. Report only what you saw or heard yourself at a timecode. Never infer a defect, or an all-clear, from the brief alone. If you are unsure whether something is a defect, report it with low confidence instead of dropping it.
7. No praise and no softening. Do not report items on the brief's "Intentional" list. Do assess every item under "Known compromises" and say whether it is acceptable.

PASS 1: PICTURE, SHOT BY SHOT

3D shots with characters:
- Face: eyes pointing somewhere sensible, eyelids, mouth, a frozen or broken expression.
- Neck and shoulders: hunched, collapsed, too wide or lumpy shoulders; a collar or jacket that deforms as the arm moves.
- Elbows, wrists, knees and fingers that bend the wrong way, twist, or pop between frames; fingers that pass through each other or do not grip.
- Feet that slide, float above the floor or sink into it; feet that are not planted while the character stands.
- Walking that glides, stutters or looks stiff; arms that do not swing; legs that cross.
- A body passing through clothes, a prop, a door, a wall or another character; skin poking through clothing; a hat or hair floating off the head.
- Held objects (phone, key, torch, crank, foam sheet) attached to the hand in every frame.
- Missing or detached shadows; a character too big or too small against the door or the boxes.

3D shots without characters, and macro shots:
- Flicker, noise, bright speckles; textures that crawl or swim; two surfaces flickering against each other.
- Objects that intersect, float or change between frames; gaps where you can see through a wall or into empty space.
- A light that jumps; reflections or shadows that are missing or wrong.
- A camera move that stutters, eases badly or stops dead; focus on the wrong thing.
- Obviously repeated texture tiles; props with nonsense writing.

3D stills and enlarged crops:
- Softness or pixelation; a push that is jerky, too fast or barely moving; film grain that stands still.

Kling AI clips:
- Hands: finger count and shape. Faces: morphing, drifting away from the cast sheet, dead eyes.
- Objects that appear, vanish, melt or merge; physics that looks wrong (stones, paper, the bag, the car).
- Walls and straight lines that wobble or "breathe"; shimmer in fine patterns (blinds, stripes, grilles).
- Nonsense text, real brand logos, readable number plates, identifiable real people.
- Things that do not belong to the period (the story runs from 2000 to 2013): phones, cars, screens, signs.

Graphics: see Pass 2. Also: an animation that stops dead before the cut, elements that pop instead of easing, anything off-centre or misaligned, inconsistent style between graphics.

Real photos (6 shots): any identifiable face; a credit line that is missing, unreadable or cut off; warping from the parallax effect (bent straight lines, smeared edges, a halo around foreground objects).

PASS 2: ON-SCREEN TEXT

Transcribe every piece of text exactly as it appears: every graphic, card, map label, counter, corner label and photo credit. Then check it against the brief's fact sheet and the narration. Report:
- spelling mistakes, wrong names, dates or numbers, inconsistent capitalisation or naming;
- text that is cut off, overlapping, off-screen, or too small to read on a phone;
- text that is on screen too briefly to read (allow about 0.3 seconds per word);
- text that appears more than about half a second before or after the words it belongs to;
- a 3D or AI shot without the two corner labels, or a graphic that wrongly has them;
- a photo without its credit.

PASS 3: CONTINUITY AND LOGIC

- State of the vault. Before the break-in: boxes shut, floor clean. After it: boxes forced open, papers on the floor. Report every shot that shows the wrong state for the moment being narrated.
- Time of day and lighting against the narration (the night of the heist, the Monday-morning discovery, the daytime visits).
- Who is on screen against who the narration is about. Check clothes, hair, age and build against the cast sheet in every 3D and AI shot, and report any character who changes between shots.
- Sets and props: the vault door, dial, key, handle, wall of boxes, police kiosk and bollards should look the same in every shot, including the AI clips.
- Reused clips (listed in the brief): is the repeat noticeable, and does the same shot stand in for two different moments in a way that misleads?

PASS 4: EDIT AND PACING

- Each cut against the words: the brief's "Says" column gives the words that belong to each shot. Report cuts that land clearly early or late.
- Picture that contradicts or ignores the narration.
- Shots too short to read (under about 1 second) and holds that sag (over about 6 seconds).
- Flash frames (a single frame of the wrong shot at a cut), black frames, freeze frames (movement stops before the cut).
- Jump cuts between similar framings; motion direction that clashes across a cut.
- Runs of three or more shots with no real movement (stills, graphics) that feel like a slideshow.
- The eight joins between parts, in picture and in sound: 00:51.92, 01:49.17, 03:11.67, 06:32.21, 07:55.04, 08:44.75, 10:10.00, 11:13.38.

PASS 5: EXPOSURE AND COLOUR

- List every shot that would be too dark to read on a phone at half brightness.
- A shot much darker, brighter, warmer or cooler than its neighbours in the same scene.
- Crushed blacks where detail matters, blown highlights, banding, heavy noise.
- Corner labels that disappear against a bright or busy background.

PASS 6: SOUND

Listen to the whole track.
- Narration: does every word match the script in the brief? Clipped starts or ends of words, unnatural gaps, doubled words, level jumps between sentences.
- Music: its level against the voice; abrupt starts and stops; an obvious loop; any moment of dead silence (give timecodes for silences longer than about 0.3 seconds).
- Effects: in sync with the action (spray, dial, key, door, grate, boxes, bags, torch); missing where the action clearly needs one; too loud; out of place.
- Clicks, pops or distortion, especially at cuts and at the eight joins.

PASS 7: AS A VIEWER

- Is the first 30 seconds a strong hook?
- Where would a viewer's attention drop? Give timecodes.
- Does the picture explain what is being said, or only decorate it?
- Against the best investigation channels, where does it fall short: motion design, visual variety, real evidence, sound design, pace? Give specific, practical suggestions.

TIMECODES

Use the video's own clock, as MM:SS.ss. Find the shot ID from the brief's shot list. If something lasts more than a moment, give its start and end. If it sits on a cut, name both shots. Say where in the frame the problem is (for example "lower left, the left hand").

SEVERITY AND CATEGORIES

Use the brief's scale: S1 Blocker, S2 Major, S3 Minor, S4 Note. Use its categories (RENDER, ANIM, AI, GFX, FACT, PHOTO, EDIT, GRADE, LABEL, AUDIO) plus CONTINUITY.

OUTPUT

Use this structure. It replaces the shorter report format in section 2 of the brief.

0. Method: how you sampled the video, and anything you could not do.
1. First impressions from the normal-speed viewing, with timecodes.
2. Coverage table, one row per shot:

If you run out of space, stop at the end of a chapter, write "CONTINUE FROM SHOT n", and carry on when asked. Do not shorten the coverage table to make it fit.
