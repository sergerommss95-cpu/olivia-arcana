# Primary-source motion research for the full-screen card field

Research date: 2026-09-22. Research only; no website files changed. All implementation choices below are proposals, not claims about how a referenced studio built its work. No runtime GPU costs were measured in this research.

## The direction these sources support

Keep the entire viewport as a continuous field of the actual 78 carved-ivory-on-lapis card images. The choreography should establish a readable near subject, a middle stream, and a quieter distant population without ending in a three-card spread. Depth must come from perspective, scale, occlusion, focus and ink darkening toward `#0a0d38`; card surfaces remain opaque. Camera and card inertia, delayed rotation, and subtle edge response are higher priorities than a blanket postprocessing stack.

## The motion rig to prototype first

These parameters and sequences are original proposals, not measured results or copied studio recipes.

1. **Separate intention from response.** A director supplies each card's target position/orientation from the overall composition. The simulation approaches that target; pointer motion modifies a force or target offset rather than directly assigning the rendered transform.
2. **Use real damped motion for settling.** Ryan Juckett's [damped-spring derivation](https://www.ryanjuckett.com/damped-springs/) gives a second-order position/velocity system, with closed-form updates and under/critical/overdamped behavior. A critically damped camera can catch up without wobbling. Give card rotation a slightly slower response than translation; use a small underdamped response only where one restrained overshoot improves the gesture.
3. **Overlap the responses.** Translation begins, tilt follows, the trailing edge flexes last; after a reversal the edge and tilt finish settling after the center. John Lasseter's original [1987 animation paper](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs/academic/class/15462-f13/www/lec_slides/Lesseter.pdf) is the foundation for anticipation, follow-through, overlap and secondary action. Applying those principles to these cards is our proposal.
4. **Anticipation is sparse.** Before an authored large reorientation, use a very small counter-tilt or compression of the field. Do not anticipate every pointer update; that would feel like latency.
5. **Keep the carved material stiff.** The visual should mostly come from rigid-body position/rotation and secondary lag. A slight trailing-edge bend is enough. Large ripples, continuous rubber wobble and strong cloth-like curling undermine the ivory relief.
6. **The field remains full-screen.** Change dominant travel direction, depth spacing or focus plane while retaining near, middle and distant cards. No three-card arrival state. Protect a stable region for any text without requiring every card to synchronize.

A frame-dependent `lerp(current,target,0.08)` is first-order smoothing and will change apparent behavior with frame rate. If smoothing is sufficient, use a delta-time coefficient `1-exp(-lambda*dt)`; for inertia and overlap retain velocity and use a spring. Clear/reset velocity history on a scene reset, context restoration, long suspension or discontinuous resize.

## Effects: benefit, implementation and cost decision

| Effect | Proposed visual use | Raw WebGL1 implementation implications | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Opaque depth ink** | Distant ivory and lapis move toward `#0a0d38` while preserving solid overlap. | Mix RGB by eye-space depth in the existing card shader. Keep card blending disabled, depth test/write enabled, and surface alpha 1. Rounded corners can be cut out geometrically or discarded; they do not require fading the entire card. | Core. Small extra arithmetic in the existing shading pass; no extra render target required. Runtime cost unmeasured. |
| **Spring rig / secondary motion** | Perceived weight, interrupted motion that remains continuous, a delayed tilt after a swipe. | Per-card position and velocity on CPU are simple at this population; upload compact state, not regenerated geometry. Analytic GPU drift can remain separate from the response solver. | Core. Establish the feel before optical effects. CPU cost must be measured. |
| **Segmented card flex** | Minimal trailing-edge movement during acceleration, zero or almost zero at rest. | Add vertices, deform in the vertex shader, and update/derive the bent normal. Four corners only give two planar triangles: they cannot create a smooth curved silhouette or interior curved geometry. Texture warping alone does not fix that. | Prototype a small segmented foreground mesh. Keep distant cards rigid if difference is invisible. |
| **Specular hairline** | A narrow warm glint crossing a bevel as orientation changes, leaving carved artwork readable. | A card-local border mask plus a virtual bevel normal and directional highlight can live in the existing fragment shader. The view/light relation must drive it; a permanent emissive outline looks like neon. | Core candidate. No bloom required. Google [Filament's material account](https://google.github.io/filament/main/filament.html#materialsystem/specularbrdf/fresnel(specularf)) supports angle-dependent specular response; our masked bevel is an art-directed approximation, not full PBR. |
| **Contact / proximity shadow** | Darken a receiving card only where another opaque card lies physically close in front of it. | A true shadow map adds a light-view pass. A local analytic projected shadow or bounded neighboring-card test is cheaper in architecture but approximate, and still must be measured. Account for actual depth separation and light direction. | Conditional. There is no contact shadow in empty space; never attach an unrelated dark halo to every floating card. A few convincing overlaps are more valuable than blanket AO. |
| **Depth of field** | A selected depth band stays sharp so the art hierarchy is intentional. Focus can travel smoothly between foreground and middle cards. | True postprocess DoF needs depth/CoC information and blur/composite work. Foreground/background handling matters at silhouettes. Blurring only the texture inside an unchanged crisp quad is texture softness, not full optical DoF. | Optional second stage. Start with ink/perspective hierarchy. If DoF is enabled, keep the chosen artwork crisp and limit blur on title-bearing cards. |
| **Velocity motion blur** | A brief directional softness during a fast sweep; barely present during idle float. | Needs current/previous object transforms and camera transforms, a velocity representation, and color gathering. Bending also needs prior deformation state. A separate velocity pass works without MRT but redraws the geometry. | Optional last stage. Useful only when displacement is visible and the improvement survives comparison. Never use global history trails as a substitute for object velocities. |

The [GPU Gems DoF chapter](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iv-image-effects/chapter-28-practical-post-process-depth-field) develops a foreground/background postprocess solution and discusses why simple blur approaches have artifacts. It supports treating focus as a depth-aware image effect; it does not supply a measured cost for our scene.

## Motion-buffer accuracy: the important trap

The [GPU Gems motion-blur chapter](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iv-image-effects/chapter-27-motion-blur-post-processing-effect) reconstructs positions from depth for camera motion and explicitly distinguishes moving objects: independent object motion needs separate velocity information. Its blur gathers color samples along the screen velocity vector. Separate geometry passes and MRT each have costs.

For this scene, derive a card vertex's current and previous clip positions from the corresponding card transforms **and** corresponding camera matrices. Perspective-divide each before finding the displacement. The previous position must use the previous bend if deformation is active. A center-only velocity misses rotation at the corners. Normalize the observed displacement by the elapsed frame time and the selected shutter duration, clamp pathological values, and clear history at discontinuities. These are proposed implementation details.

Do not mistake chromatic separation, duplicated translucent cards, whole-frame feedback, a radial camera smear, or soft image sampling for accurate per-card velocity blur. They can be artistic effects, but they have different failure modes and would erode the requested opacity/material character.

## Raw WebGL1 capability and geometry constraints

- [ANGLE_instanced_arrays](https://registry.khronos.org/webgl/extensions/ANGLE_instanced_arrays/) supplies instanced draws and attribute divisors to WebGL1. It is an extension, so feature-test it and retain a normal-draw or merged-geometry fallback. Instancing works with segmented meshes; it is not restricted to four-vertex quads.
- [WEBGL_draw_buffers](https://registry.khronos.org/webgl/extensions/WEBGL_draw_buffers/) supplies multiple color outputs. Without it, color and velocity cannot simply be emitted to two render targets in one draw. A separate velocity pass is the compatible architecture. When available, RGBA/UNSIGNED_BYTE attachment combinations have specified guarantees; test framebuffer completeness.
- [WEBGL_depth_texture](https://registry.khronos.org/webgl/extensions/WEBGL_depth_texture/) enables sampling depth textures in WebGL1. A depth renderbuffer used for testing is not automatically a sampleable texture. A color-encoded depth pass is a fallback, with extra work.
- Core [WebGL 1.0.3](https://registry.khronos.org/webgl/specs/1.0.3/) follows GLSL ES 1.00 constraints. Use compile-time blur loop bounds and do not depend on WebGL2 texture arrays, integer vertex IDs, core MRT, or core instancing. Query attribute and texture limits on the actual context. Two per-instance `mat4` values already consume eight attribute slots before geometry/UV/atlas data; compact transforms or a different pass layout may be needed.
- [OES_standard_derivatives](https://registry.khronos.org/webgl/extensions/OES_standard_derivatives/) enables fragment derivatives used for stable procedural edge widths. Feature-test it; otherwise supply an approximate projected pixel scale. Do not assume `fwidth` is universally core in WebGL1.
- A 6-by-10 **segment** plane has 77 vertices and 120 triangles. Across 78 cards this is 6,006 vertices and 9,360 triangles per geometry pass; a four-vertex plane has 4 vertices/2 triangles per card. These counts are arithmetic, **not performance measurements**. The visible artwork area and postprocess sampling may dominate before these vertex counts do.
- The 78 different images need an atlas or batching strategy for instancing. Pad atlas tiles and their mip levels to prevent neighboring-card bleeding. Several smaller atlas batches can be preferable to a single texture exceeding the device's limit; decide using actual image dimensions and queried limits.

## Cost model and verification contract

No frame-time figures are asserted. The figures below are storage/sample arithmetic, not observed GPU allocation or performance:

- A 1920×1080 RGBA8 image contains 8,294,400 bytes, approximately 7.91 MiB, before driver overhead. A same-size color target plus velocity target doubles that color storage. A 16-bit depth attachment adds approximately 3.96 MiB. Additional blur targets, the default framebuffer and any multisampling are extra.
- Halving **both** dimensions gives one quarter the pixel count. It also lowers velocity/depth-edge fidelity, so compare thin silhouettes carefully.
- A fixed eight-tap fullscreen gather at 1920×1080 implies roughly 16.6 million color sample requests per frame, before velocity/depth reads, cache behavior and optimizations. This does not imply a particular millisecond cost.
- Doubling drawing-buffer DPR multiplies pixel count by four. A DPR cap or dynamic render scale is often more relevant to fullscreen effects than the difference between 4 and 77 vertices per card, but that is a hypothesis to test here.

Measure the baseline and each effect individually with the same camera/scene/input trace. Report median and high-percentile frame duration, dropped frames, drawing-buffer dimensions, active card count, device/browser, and warm-up. Use [EXT_disjoint_timer_query](https://registry.khronos.org/webgl/extensions/EXT_disjoint_timer_query/) for asynchronous GPU timing where available and discard disjoint samples. JavaScript callback duration is not a GPU time measurement. Test actual mobile hardware as well as desktop, and examine a recorded fast sweep plus a still frame at rest.

Recommended quality order: preserve real artwork and opacity → motion rig → depth/color composition → restrained bevel glint → only then selective contact shadows and DoF → velocity blur after those pass visual/performance comparisons. Reduced-motion mode should hold a composed full-screen field and avoid continuing pointer-driven spring oscillation or blur.
