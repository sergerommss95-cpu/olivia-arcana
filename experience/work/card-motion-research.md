# Card-handling research for Olivia Arcana

Research date: 22 September 2026. Scope: a full-screen scroll hero, with lapis/ivory cards floating against an abyss. This is a motion-reference brief, not implementation. No fixed small stage and no required PAST/NOW/NEXT ending.

Evidence boundary: instructor-authored prose, published tutorial transcripts, and official tutorial descriptions were checked. The videos below are suitable visual references, but this research did not measure them frame by frame. Mechanics below are qualitative; suitability and translation choices are authored design judgments.

## Recommended motion vocabulary

For the intended quiet, magical mood, prioritize a single-card separation/deal, a broad overlapping ribbon, a restrained fan, and one traveling turnover. These offer a clear silhouette and slow revelation of the existing artwork. Treat pressure releases, elaborate cuts and airborne catches as optional reference material; reproducing their full sequences would suggest a dexterity performance.

Cardistry itself is a visual performance practice, not synonymous with deceptive magic. Andrei Jikh's [Cardistry 101](https://store.theory11.com/products/cardistry-101-andrei-jikh/1000) explicitly frames it as non-magical manipulation. The hero can borrow physical cues while deliberately suspending gravity; name that translation honestly.

## Named moves, mechanics, and translation

| Move | Supported handling description | Application to this hero — design judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Single-card deal / low glide | In the draw deal, the top card is pushed aside, taken by the other hand, carried diagonally, and placed. A stud deal adds a controlled turnover. Giobbi stresses light, quiet handling. [Roberto Giobbi, Introduction to Card Magic, pp. 37–39](https://www.eugenemagicclub.com/Giobbi_Card_Magic-Intro.pdf) | Strong fit. Preserve the sequence: separation before travel, slight tilt before face revelation, quiet arrival. “Low glide” describes its appearance; it is not the technical name of a flourish. |
| Table spread | A deck is progressively distributed over a surface in overlapping increments. School of Cardistry teaches it as a basic spread. [Original Table Spread tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0gGONQKuM5s) | Strong fit as a reference for ordered emergence. In the hero, replace the table with a coherent implied floating plane and retain consistent overlap. Avoid calling independently flying cards a physical table spread. |
| Ribbon spread | This is commonly the name for a controlled table spread, straight or curved; these are not necessarily separate techniques. A beveled deck and even pressure create a regular ribbon. [Michael Patrick's Ribbon Spread transcript](https://howcast.com/videos/515098-how-to-do-a-ribbon-spread-magic-card-flourishes/), [52Kards Ribbon Spread and Flip](https://52kards.com/video/ribbon-spread/) | Best fit for a broad full-screen sweep. The leading deck moves while earlier cards remain exposed. Preserve identity, order and progressive release; do not have every card begin its journey at once. |
| Ribbon turnover | One end of the overlapping spread is lifted and guided across, causing a traveling turnover. Patrick explicitly identifies surface friction as necessary and notes that gaps can break the chain. [Same Ribbon Spread transcript](https://howcast.com/videos/515098-how-to-do-a-ribbon-spread-magic-card-flourishes/) | Strong fit for one magical reveal: a narrow wave of local rotation travels through the ribbon. A floating version has supernatural support, but should preserve neighboring overlap and a legible leading edge. Avoid synchronized full-deck flipping or cloth-like ripples through each card. |
| Arm spread | Cards are beveled and spread evenly along the forearm; a turnover and scoop-up can follow. Uneven gaps interrupt the turnover. [Michael Patrick's Arm Spread transcript](https://howcast.com/videos/515123-how-to-do-an-arm-spread-magic-card-flourishes/), [School of Cardistry original tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XhvD5h4sVBk) | Borrow the narrow supported ribbon and gathering action, not the literal arm/catch. Without an arm it is better described as a suspended ribbon inspired by an arm spread. Throwing and catching pushes the mood toward performance spectacle. |
| Riffle shuffle + bridge | Two packets are flexed and their edges released to interlace. The interlaced deck is bowed, then pressure is eased so the bridge settles into one deck. [Michael Patrick's in-hands riffle/bridge transcript](https://howcast.com/videos/515112-in-hands-riffle-shuffle-w-bridge-finish-magic-card-flourishes/), [52Kards tabled version](https://52kards.com/video/tabled-bridge-riffle-shuffle/) | Medium fit. Use a clear build/release/settle phrase if shuffling has narrative purpose. Do not equate a riffle with a perfect one-by-one faro. The bridge has stored bend and release; a uniform loose-card orbit misses that cause. |
| Faro / weave | Two aligned packets interweave at their edges under controlled pressure. A perfect faro has exact alternating cards; ordinary demonstration need not be mathematically perfect. [Michael Patrick's Faro transcript](https://howcast.com/videos/515110-how-to-do-the-faro-shuffle-magic-card-flourishes/), [Alex Melnik / December Boys tutorial](https://decemberboys.com.ua/en/tutorials/faro/) | Medium fit as a precise, short moment of joining. Keep the two packet identities until edges engage; avoid solid cards passing through one another. A floating “weave” can be abstracted without implying a real perfect shuffle. |
| Cascade | The name is used for more than one finish. Patrick teaches a descending cascade after a woven shuffle, released toward the receiving hand; other instructors call the bridge finish a waterfall/cascade. [Michael Patrick's Card Cascade transcript](https://howcast.com/videos/515108-how-to-do-a-card-cascade-magic-card-flourishes/), [52Kards tabled bridge](https://52kards.com/video/tabled-bridge-riffle-shuffle/) | Specify which reference is meant: bowed bridge settling or descending woven cascade. Do not use “cascade,” “dribble,” and “spring” as interchangeable mechanics. A paused cascade can become an attractive suspended arc, but its pause is authored magic. |
| Spring | Flexed cards are released so they shoot sequentially toward a receiving hand. Release grip and direction vary. Patrick teaches coordinated release from opposing contact areas; TheRussianGenius teaches pressure and paced individual release. [Michael Patrick Spring transcript](https://howcast.com/videos/515121-how-to-do-a-spring-magic-card-flourishes/), [TheRussianGenius Spring](https://therussiangenius.vhx.tv/products/card-spring) | Lower fit as a literal full move: it reads as energetic propulsion. Borrow a single-file trajectory and release lag, with a brief visible loading phase. Extremely slow hovering is an art-directed reinterpretation, not an unmodified real spring. |
| Dribble | Cards fall sequentially or in very small packets from the held deck into a lower hand or onto a table. Giobbi describes light pressure, delicate release and an even pace. [Giobbi, pp. 88–90](https://www.eugenemagicclub.com/Giobbi_Card_Magic-Intro.pdf), [Vanishing Inc. Dribble](https://www.vanishingincmagic.com/card-magic-basics/dribble/) | Medium fit as soft descent or a settling phrase. Differentiate it from the more strongly propelled spring. A long uniform floating strand is not the natural outcome of a dribble; make the magical suspension a conscious design choice. |
| Charlier cut | A one-handed two-packet cut: one packet is released down, raised by the index finger until it clears the other, then the packets close in exchanged order. [Michael Patrick Charlier transcript](https://howcast.com/videos/515119-how-to-do-a-charlier-cut-magic-card-flourishes/), [School of Cardistry original tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YdBdW0OlLpQ) | Medium fit if reduced to two clearly related floating packets. Keep cards inside each packet aligned and preserve a plausible clearance path. Useful as a deliberate opening/closing gesture; less useful as continuous hero motion. |
| Sybil | Chris Kenner's influential multi-packet cut has many variations. 52Kards teaches a false version restoring deck order; its Vinh Giang variation is explicitly described as a multi-packet display. [52Kards Sybil](https://52kards.com/video/card-tricks-sybil-cut-tutorial/), [Vinh Giang's variation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C4jeGdmXvmI), [Kenner's artist page](https://www.theory11.com/about/artists/chris-kenner) | Low fit for the main quiet hero. Many simultaneous pivots, crossings and packet handoffs read as technical virtuosity. Borrow one open geometric pose only if needed. Do not claim every Sybil variant is false or has the same packet count. |
| Thumb fan | The deck is spread into a wide arc through controlled pressure and a supported pivot. Size and closure are teachable refinements, not a prescribed universal angle. [dealersgrip's original Thumb Fan tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fZqLvpgyePY), [December Boys Thumb Fan](https://decemberboys.com.ua/en/tutorials/thumb-fan/), [52Kards beginner guide](https://shop.52kards.com/blogs/posts/beginners-guide-to-learning-cardistry-v2) | Strong fit. An anchored curved opening creates order and reveals repeated borders. Preserve overlap and a recognizable pivot; allow an authored opening angle chosen for artwork and composition. Avoid claiming every thumb fan spans 300°. |
| Pressure fan / spring fan | Different mechanism from a thumb fan: spring pressure releases the cards around an arc. Patrick notes that his handling is difficult to execute slowly. [Tom Interval's practitioner explanation](https://intervalmagic.wordpress.com/2022/04/19/how-to-fan-a-deck-of-cards-the-pressure-fan/), [Michael Patrick Pressure Fan transcript](https://howcast.com/videos/515101-how-to-do-the-pressure-fan-magic-card-flourishes/) | Medium fit. Use the swelling fan silhouette, with a perceptible compression-to-opening relationship. For a very slow hero, label it pressure-fan-inspired rather than a literal slow-motion physical technique. |
| Giant / double-tier fan | Patrick starts from a partially woven faro arrangement and opens both interleaved tiers; closure can strip the packets apart or finish with a bridge. [Michael Patrick Giant Fan transcript](https://howcast.com/videos/515102-how-to-do-the-giant-fan-magic-card-flourishes/) | Strong silhouette, medium complexity. It is not merely a bigger thumb fan or a deck scaled up. Preserve two visible nested tiers. Consider a brief hero-wide display, then simplify before the composition becomes dense. |

## Corrections and physics boundaries

- **“The Glide” is a named sleight.** 52Kards describes it as a secret card-switching method. Use “deal,” “single-card slide,” or explicitly visual “glide” for hero travel. [The Glide](https://52kards.com/video/the-glide/)
- **No universal 300° thumb fan.** The checked practitioner material does not establish that as a universal requirement. Fan extent varies with handling, pivot, deck, card count and the desired display. If 300° is chosen, mark it as an authored composition target and test card overlap; do not report it as a measured norm.
- **Bending anisotropy is real; its alignment with card shape is not automatic.** BGPPL's playing-card board specification reports different machine-direction and cross-direction bending stiffness. Lexmark explains that grain is fiber alignment and may run along a sheet's length or width. Those sources support direction-dependent behavior, not a universal “cards bend easier around their long axis” rule. The actual card stock, lamination and cut orientation must be known. [BGPPL playing-card board](https://www.bgppl.com/bgppl-imperial-c2s-ab-pcb-%E2%80%93-playing-card-board-detail-5-1), [Lexmark card stock/grain guide](https://publications.lexmark.com/publications/lexmark_hardware/Paper_and_Specialty_Media_Guide/html/en/tips-on-using-card-stock-topic.html)
- **Name the bend direction unambiguously.** “Curvature varying along the long dimension” means the bend axis runs across the short dimension. “Bend about the long axis” means curvature varies across the width. These phrases must not be swapped. Dimensions and material direction are separate variables.
- **No physical constants were established for Olivia's cards.** Do not invent mass, modulus, drag, friction, restitution, torsional stiffness, or a long/short stiffness ratio. A supplier's values for one board are not measurements of the illustrated tarot deck. Use qualitative stiffness and restraint unless the actual stock is measured.
- **Preserve object character.** As design judgment, let the cards read as thin, mostly flat sheets with limited broad curvature. Packets stay coherent; single cards flex gently and recover. Persistent cloth waves, rubber stretching, arbitrary tumbling and repeated bounces would weaken that reading.
- **Sources do not prescribe animation duration.** Any seconds, pixels, degrees, lag, scroll distance or easing in the eventual specification must be labeled authored starting values. They can be tuned for perceived calm and legibility, without presenting them as measurements from demonstrations.

## Source-use notes

The primary tutorial pages are more reliable for mechanics than search snippets from forums. Some 52Kards pages contain unrelated injected/spam text and comment spam; only the identified instructor description was used. No claims are derived from those unrelated sections. The Giobbi PDF is a primary authored text hosted by Eugene Magic Club; its [author's product page](https://www.robertogiobbi.com/site/product/roberto-giobbis-introduction-to-card-magic/) establishes the original work. No paywalled lesson contents were inferred from a course index.

For the final motion brief, use a small number of carefully selected motifs rather than a demonstration reel of every technique above. The strongest candidate is: poised deck → individual separation → broad overlapping fan/ribbon → one traveling face reveal → calm open composition. This sequence is an authored recommendation, not a required ending or a reconstruction of a cardist's routine.
